The 24–48 hours that mattered

Pentagon personnel database breach exposes Social Security numbers of 3 million

A breach of the Defense Manpower Data Center exposed sensitive records — including Social Security numbers, dates of birth and military occupational details — for roughly 2.76 million living individuals and 294,000 deceased people. Unauthorized access reportedly ran from October 2025 until the flaw was discovered in July 2026; the Pentagon says it has no evidence yet the data has been misused and is offering affected individuals credit monitoring.

Help Net Security

New NetScaler zero-day added to CISA's must-patch list

Citrix issued emergency patches for CVE-2026-88779, an unauthenticated memory-overflow flaw in NetScaler ADC and Gateway (CVSS 8.7) that attackers are using to knock SAML-based single sign-on deployments offline. CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 4 with a remediation deadline of October 7 — a separate issue from the webshell-planting NetScaler bugs disclosed earlier this week.

The Hacker News

Rejetto HTTP File Server flaw under active attack

Exploitation attempts began October 1, a day after researchers published details of CVE-2026-61500 (CVSS 9.3), a weak pseudo-random number generator in the long-popular Rejetto HFS that lets attackers forge admin sessions and achieve remote code execution. Anyone still running HFS should patch or pull internet-facing instances offline immediately.

Teen suspect behind KillSec ransomware arrested, leak site seized

Spanish police arrested a 16-year-old suspected of running KillSec, a ransomware-as-a-service operation accused of stealing data from victim organizations and threatening to publish it unless paid, and seized its leak site and servers in the process.

The Hacker News

FBI makes its first-ever "cyber fugitive" arrest off its Top 10 Most Wanted list

Venezuelan national Anibal "Prometheus" Canelon Aguirre, alleged developer of the Ploutus ATM jackpotting malware, has been captured and appeared in a U.S. federal court. Prosecutors say his crew stole more than $5.4 million from ATMs across dozens of jackpotting incidents and laundered proceeds through the Tren de Aragua gang; he faces bank fraud, money laundering and computer fraud charges carrying up to 30 years.

BleepingComputer

Microsoft's official X account hijacked to push a fake "Clippy" crypto token

Attackers took over @Microsoft on X for roughly 30 minutes on October 1, swapping the profile picture and posting promotions for a fraudulent $Clippy token to the account's 13 million followers. Microsoft confirmed the breach, removed the posts and said it has no connection to the token — a reminder that even a brief takeover of a high-trust verified account can be weaponized for a scam.

Anthropic's IPO filing reveals a $518 billion decade-long infrastructure bet

A confidential prospectus reviewed by Reuters shows Anthropic plans to spend at least $518 billion over the next decade on cloud and data-center capacity across six partners — roughly $111B with Google, $110B with Amazon and $31B with Microsoft, plus $161B in largely non-cancelable Broadcom equipment leases — with about 80% of the commitments owed regardless of usage. A public listing could follow the November midterms at a valuation north of $2 trillion.

Nvidia: Anthropic's contracted compute now tops $180 billion

Nvidia disclosed that Anthropic's total contracted cloud and "neocloud" capacity using its chips has surpassed $180 billion, including 2.5 gigawatts locked in for delivery by 2028 and a separate $35 billion deal with Lambda for a Texas data center. The figure illustrates how concentrated — and capital-intensive — the AI buildout has become for a handful of frontier labs.

AMD buys Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2 billion

AMD agreed to acquire spatial-intelligence startup World Labs in an all-stock deal, bringing founder Fei-Fei Li on board as AMD's chief scientist reporting directly to CEO Lisa Su. World Labs builds models that generate and simulate interactive 3D environments for robotics and physical-AI applications — AMD's second-largest acquisition on record, expected to close by year end.

Trump launches "Super Intelligence Force" AI task force

President Trump announced a new White House task force, chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton alongside FTC, Pentagon research and OPM leads, tasked with assessing AI risks and opportunities and auditing government systems within 120 days. Clayton, effectively the administration's new AI czar, framed the effort around maintaining U.S. dominance: "The risk of not being first is high."

OpenAI's safety transparency lead resigns, warns of a "fundamentally broken" culture

David Robinson, who led OpenAI's safety system-card and transparency work, has resigned days after the company fired three safety researchers and months after dissolving its Preparedness team. In an Atlantic essay he argued Silicon Valley's culture of relentless shipping and overconfidence needs far greater humility and safeguards — the latest in a string of safety-team departures from the company.

Meta's Muse Spark AI helps mathematicians crack six research problems

Meta says its Muse Spark models, used through the ordinary Meta.ai chat interface in "Thinking Mode," collaborated with mathematicians on six papers spanning probability, group theory and optimization — five of which resolve previously open questions, including disproving a 2024 conjecture about semiabelian groups with a 384-element counterexample.
