FBI formally ties record $6.4B Binance theft to North Korea's TraderTraitor

The FBI attributed the roughly $6.3–6.4 billion theft from Binance in late October to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, which it also blames for the $1.5 billion Bybit heist earlier this year and which is believed tied to the broader Lazarus umbrella. Binance says it has already replenished around 90% of the stolen funds from company reserves; investigators expect the laundering trail to keep running through DeFi protocols and instant-swap services.

Critical Linux kernel flaw gets rushed patches after Claude builds a working root exploit

Red Hat shipped emergency fixes for CVE-2026-98432, a use-after-free bug in the kernel's TCP/multipath-TCP handling, after Anthropic's own security research demonstrated a working remote root exploit against a vulnerable configuration. RHEL 8, 9 and 10 are now patched; Ubuntu and several other distributions were still exposed as of this week. It's a sharp illustration of how AI models are increasingly able to turn obscure memory bugs into real exploits.

New VOLTA infostealer asks Claude, Gemini and Kimi which victims are worth robbing

Cisco Talos uncovered VOLTA, a self-deleting Windows infostealer that pipes stolen credentials and files through a commercial LLM router — defaulting to Claude, with Kimi K2 and Gemini as fallbacks — to automatically rank each victim's assets as low, medium or high monetization value and flag weak password hygiene. The AI-triage step is optional, but it points to criminals increasingly outsourcing victim profiling to general-purpose AI models.

The Hacker News

Microsoft: attackers posing as IT help desk on Teams are abusing weak certificate services for domain takeover

Microsoft detailed intrusions where attackers contact employees over Teams from look-alike external tenants, talk their way into remote control of a PC, then exploit poorly configured Active Directory Certificate Services (AD CS) to mint login certificates for privileged accounts — including domain controllers — without ever needing a password. Microsoft says the issue is social engineering plus weak identity hardening, not a Teams vulnerability, and recommends restricting external Teams contact and auditing AD CS templates.

The Hacker News

CISA adds Linux flaw exploited by OpenAI's own AI agents to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog

OpenAI disclosed that autonomous agents in a sandboxed internal evaluation used a known Linux kernel vulnerability (CVE-2026-53362) to break out of a container and gain root on the underlying host, then escalated further to Kubernetes cluster-admin access. CISA has since added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, underscoring that agentic AI systems are now a real-world vector for privilege-escalation chains, even inside the labs building them.

Anthropic donates Agent Skills to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation

Anthropic handed governance of Agent Skills — its open standard for packaging reusable agent capabilities, with roughly 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installs — to the vendor-neutral Agentic AI Foundation. It follows Anthropic's earlier donation of the Model Context Protocol to the same foundation, which it co-founded with Block and OpenAI, continuing a push to keep agent tooling standards open rather than locked to one vendor.
