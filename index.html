Cisco patches Secure Email Gateway zero-day under active attack

Cisco disclosed a path-traversal flaw (CVE-2026-76519, CVSS 9.8) in Secure Email Gateway's malware-scanning feature that lets attackers send a crafted attachment to gain root-level command execution, and said its own PSIRT found it being exploited in the wild. CISA added the bug to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day; it's the second Secure Email Gateway zero-day Cisco has confirmed under attack this autumn.

A proof-of-concept for CVE-2026-21589 (CVSS 9.3), an unauthenticated flaw letting remote attackers read arbitrary files in Confluence, Jira and Bitbucket Data Center deployments, triggered exploitation attempts almost immediately after it went public. Researchers have logged scanning from dozens of IPs across more than ten countries; admins running self-hosted Atlassian products are urged to patch now rather than wait for a formal KEV listing.

A cybersecurity incident at British defense contractor BAE Systems knocked out the system federal bankruptcy courts use to issue creditor notices nationwide, delaying filings and disrupting court operations. Police dismantle KillSec ransomware gang allegedly run by a 16-year-old

"Operation KillSwitch," a German-led international investigation into roughly 1,000 suspected KillSec ransomware attacks worldwide, ended with Spanish police arresting a 16-year-old suspected of administering the group, plus two more arrests in the UK and Romania. Investigators seized five servers and more than 110TB of stolen data; a US grand jury has separately indicted a UK-based suspect who now faces extradition.

CISA added CVE-2026-0322, a code-injection vulnerability in Palo Alto Networks' PAN-OS, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog this week after confirming active exploitation, giving federal civilian agencies until October 17 to remediate. Eight US states sue OpenAI over ChatGPT's safety marketing to minors

Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia filed a coordinated bipartisan lawsuit alleging OpenAI misrepresented ChatGPT's safety while failing to adequately protect minors and other vulnerable users, including claims the bot can reinforce delusions. The states want court-ordered product changes, damages, and in one proposed remedy, a bar on OpenAI building self-improving models until alignment can be demonstrated; it's described as the most significant state action yet against an AI company.

At an October 13 hearing before a UK Commons committee, OpenAI and Anthropic told MPs that voluntary commitments aren't enough and called for statutory, binding AI safety rules starting with mandatory incident reporting. Google DeepMind and Meta took a more cautious line, favoring technical standards and existing regulators over new binding legislation, leaving the four leading labs publicly split on how far UK law should go.
