CISA adds four actively exploited flaws to its watch list — OpenSSH "SSHole" among them

On November 9, CISA added four exploited vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: an OpenSSH authentication bypass on musl-based Linux without PAM (CVE-2026-123158, dubbed "SSHole," reportedly abused within hours of disclosure), a 9.8-severity code-injection flaw in FreeSWITCH, a bug in the GPUStack AI-infrastructure platform, and an incorrect-authorization flaw in IBM Aspera Faspex. Federal civilian agencies had until November 12 to patch.

SonicWall SSLVPN flaw lets attackers defeat MFA — now on CISA's exploited list

CISA added CVE-2026-19868 to its KEV catalog on November 6: a SonicOS SSLVPN authentication-enforcement flaw that can bypass SAML-based login controls, including multi-factor authentication, on Gen7 and Gen8 SonicWall firewalls. Federal agencies have until November 20 to apply mitigations; admins running affected builds should check SonicWall's advisory now.

Debian and Ubuntu patch a bug that could turn a retried download into root access

Debian, Ubuntu and the APT project shipped fixes for CVE-2026-109518, a flaw that can let an attacker reach root-level code execution when APT retries a failed package download under specific conditions. Exploitation isn't trivial, but patched versions are already out, so a prompt system update closes the hole.

Hijacked update channel pushes a backdoor to nearly 3,000 servers

OpsShield says an attacker used stolen credentials — not a software bug — to break into the update-distribution infrastructure for its cPGuard hosting-security product on November 2 and push a tampered update that harvested password hashes, database configs and SSH files. Roughly 2,950 servers running affected versions (5.88.12–5.88.19) pulled the malicious update before it was caught; OpsShield is telling customers to patch and rotate any exposed credentials.

OpenAI's split with Cursor takes effect, cutting a major coding tool off its models

As of November 12, OpenAI's models are no longer directly available inside Cursor, the AI coding editor SpaceX acquired earlier this year. OpenAI said in August it couldn't be confident SpaceX would keep its technology within OpenAI's terms of service, citing past breaches at X and xAI. Cursor users can still reach OpenAI's models with their own API keys, alongside the Anthropic, Google and xAI models Cursor continues to offer natively.

China's Moonshot AI published open weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro on November 4, a 2.8-trillion-parameter, million-token-context model that ranks among the largest open-weight releases to date.
