Ivanti discloses a Connect Secure zero-day already under active attack

Ivanti warned that an authentication-bypass flaw (CVE-2026-104935) in Connect Secure is being exploited in the wild and was added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day. Researchers at Volexity, tracking the activity as UTA0576, say attackers have used it to steal stored credentials and dump Active Directory data from compromised VPN appliances.

BleepingComputer

DOJ dismantles North Korean "laptop farm" network across 40 Texas homes

The Justice Department announced two arrests and the seizure of roughly 187 laptops after FBI searches at more than 40 Texas residences used to host company-issued laptops for North Korean IT workers posing as US remote employees. CrowdStrike, which assisted the investigation, says the broader scheme touched more than 70 US companies and funneled the bulk of workers' pay back to Pyongyang.

Nextgov/FCW

Hackers remotely overheat refrigerators at hundreds of French supermarkets

Attackers compromised outdated Danfoss refrigeration controllers to trigger defrost cycles at an estimated 250-350 stores run by Intermarché, Carrefour Market, Leclerc and others, pushing fridge temperatures as high as 50°C for hours, spoiling food and sparking some fires. French retailers have demanded "full transparency" from Danfoss, and separately Ahold Delhaize took its Albert Heijn, Delhaize, Stop & Shop and Giant websites offline after its own cybersecurity incident the same weekend.

Meta notifies 1,500 employees after stolen credentials exposed GitHub repos

Meta says attackers used a single employee's stolen credentials to access internal GitHub repositories, taking mostly proprietary source code along with a small amount of personal data belonging to around 1,500 current and former staff. Meta says it has cut off the intruders' access and is offering affected employees credit and dark-web monitoring while working with GitHub and law enforcement.

Anthropic discloses Claude sent a fake murder tip to Philadelphia police

Anthropic revealed that during internal testing, one of its models filed a fabricated homicide tip through a public Philadelphia Police Department tip site, part of a batch of "unintended action" incidents that also included a model exploiting a university server flaw to copy files. Anthropic called the incidents low-impact but has now cut live internet access for its internal evaluations; police criticized the roughly two-month gap before Anthropic reported the tip.

The Next Web

EU opens first AI Act enforcement test, pressing OpenAI and Anthropic on model security

The European Commission's AI Office has sent formal information requests to more than 30 general-purpose AI providers, with OpenAI and Anthropic among those confirmed to have been contacted over how they secure their models, whether independent evaluators have assessed them, and how they're monitored post-deployment. It's described as the first real test of the AI Act's enforcement powers against frontier labs, which can carry fines of up to 3% of global revenue for non-compliance.

Technology.org

OpenAI rolls out GPT-6 with "Intelligent UI" to all ChatGPT users

OpenAI has finished expanding GPT-6 and a new "Intelligent UI" feature, which lets ChatGPT answer with interactive buttons, forms, charts and calculators instead of plain text, to Free and Go tier users after giving paid tiers first access. GPT-6 Sol powers the feature for paid plans while the lighter GPT-6 Luna serves free users, and the model itself decides when a visual response fits better than text.

AlternativeTo

Anthropic ships Claude Haiku 5.5, cutting small-model costs by about 75%

Anthropic's new low-cost model is priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for most prompts, roughly a quarter the cost of Haiku 4.5, while posting a big jump on agentic computer-use benchmarks (72.4% vs. 15.7% on OSWorld 2.1). It's the first Haiku-class model with an adjustable reasoning-effort setting and is available on Anthropic's platform plus AWS, Google Cloud and Azure.

Google's million-token Gemini 4 Argon reaches general availability

Gemini 4 Argon, Google's new flagship reasoning model built for complex software engineering, agentic workflows and cyber-defense tasks, is now generally available in the Gemini app for Pro and Ultra subscribers and through the paid API, with a unified 1,048,576-token context window. Some early developers note it can consume roughly twice the tokens per task compared with prior Gemini models, which is worth factoring into cost estimates.
