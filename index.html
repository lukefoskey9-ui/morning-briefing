North Korean "WaterPlum" fake-recruiter campaign infected 30,000 devices, stole $10.7M in crypto

A joint advisory from law enforcement and intelligence agencies in the US, Japan, Australia, and Germany detailed a North Korean operation that lured developers and Web3 specialists with fake job interviews, compromising roughly 30,000 devices across more than 100 countries and around 7,000 crypto wallets between December 2025 and July 2026. Victims were asked to download bogus "coding assignments" that delivered the malware.

The Register

Researchers escape OpenAI Codex's sandbox two different ways, one requiring zero clicks

Security researchers disclosed two techniques — "Heapjack" and "Overpatch" — that broke out of Codex's sandboxing. Heapjack, the more severe of the two, let a malicious repository gain unsandboxed code execution on a victim's machine merely by having Codex asked a question about that repo's code. OpenAI fixed both within eight days of the August 12 report.

Malicious npm package with 2 million downloads hid its payload in a runtime method, not the install script

Checkmarx researchers found that "indexed-btree," a package impersonating the legitimate sorted-btree library, avoided npm's install-script scanning entirely by hiding its loader inside a normal runtime method instead. Once triggered, it exfiltrates system data via Slack and Telegram and polls an Ethereum smart contract for second-stage commands.

Clinicians are resisting AI beyond diagnostics, the Financial Times reports

The FT reports doctors accept AI for imaging and diagnostics but are pushing back hard on tools for documentation, treatment recommendations, and patient communication, citing thin evidence of real-world benefit. A Wolters Kluwer survey of 355 US clinicians found daily AI use tripled year over year, yet 74% distrust outputs due to hallucinations and only 27% are aware of formal governance policies at their institution.
