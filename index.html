Attackers exploit new OpenSSH flaw within hours of disclosure

A newly disclosed OpenSSH vulnerability dubbed "SSHole" (CVE-2026-123158) lets attackers log into certain password-disabled accounts on Alpine Linux and other musl-based systems simply by submitting an oversized password. Researchers observed exploitation attempts within hours of the advisory going public; OpenSSH 10.6p2 fixes the issue and should be applied immediately.

mSeven Software disclosed that an attacker breached its mSecure password manager and stole an encrypted customer database. The company is urging all users to change their master passwords, reset two-factor authentication, and revoke and reissue passkeys as a precaution.

Google and Mandiant warned that attackers are increasingly stealing or abusing enterprise access to large language models to run their own workloads on the victim's tab, with fraudulent cloud charges at some organizations reaching as much as $1 million.
